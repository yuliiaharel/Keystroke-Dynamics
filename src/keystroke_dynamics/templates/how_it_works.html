What is keystroke dynamics?

Keystroke dynamics is a behavioral biometric: the detailed timing of when each key is pressed and released as someone types. Two raw signals are measured for each key: hold time (how long a key is held down) and flight time (the gap between releasing one key and pressing the next). A pair of consecutive keys and the timing between them is called a digraph.

How this app works

When you create a profile, the browser records the hold time for each key and the flight time between consecutive key pairs you type. For each distinct key (or key pair), the server keeps a running mean and variance using Welford's online algorithm — so your profile improves with every sample without ever storing the raw timestamps it was built from.

To authenticate, the app extracts the same features from your new sample and computes a scaled Manhattan distance between each feature and your stored mean, scaled by that feature's own standard deviation. The scores are averaged into a single distance; if it is at or below a calibrated threshold, the sample is a match.

Measured accuracy and limits

The scoring method was evaluated against a public keystroke-timing benchmark dataset, using its published train/test split. It reached an equal error rate (EER) of about 9% — close to the ~10% reported in the dataset's original publication for comparable methods. An EER this high means the method is a reasonable research-grade demonstration, not a secure standalone authenticator: in roughly 1 in 11 genuine attempts it would be scored as a non-match, and roughly as often an impostor would be scored as a match.

Because of this, typing is used only as one signal alongside your password, never as a replacement for it. A failed typing check is shown to you but never fails your login or locks your account by itself. Until a profile has enough data, the app reports insufficient data rather than guessing.
